    The exception

    Upstream code in ParanoidBSD is not relicensed

    ParanoidBSD vendors two upstream trees and ports from both. hbsd/ stays under HardenedBSD’s and FreeBSD’s own terms; kde/ stays under KDE’s. Neither can be relicensed and Imortek does not claim to. Ported files under pbsd/ keep the upstream copyright header they inherited, and that header governs the file — it beats the per-path default.

    LICENSING.md is the authoritative per-path map, and docs/PROVENANCE.md records which modules are ported and which are new PBSD work, so the boundary is checkable rather than asserted. New work — the capability nucleus, the tooling, the compositor and the Aero theme — carries the Imortek dual licence.

    Three carve-outs the audit turned up. Twenty-two ported files inherit the original four-clause BSD licence, whose advertising clause the AGPL cannot accept, so they are excluded from the AGPL grant and must not enter an AGPL binary. pbsd/zfs/ derives from OpenZFS, which is CDDL and has no compatibility route to the AGPL — ZFS stays a separate unmodified module. And the KDE-derived work is compatible only through the “or later” clause, so GPL-2.0-only code must never enter the tree. All three are listed in NOTICES.md and enforced by a CI check.

    Questions people actually ask

    Before you email

    We are a startup under AUD 50,000. Do we really pay nothing?
    Correct — you use it under AGPL-3.0+ for free. The obligations still apply: publish your modifications under the same dual licence, keep the attribution line, and be aware that offering it over a network counts as distribution under AGPL. When you cross the threshold, talk to us then.
    What does “network use triggers source obligations” actually mean?
    The AGPL closes the gap the GPL leaves open. Under the GPL, running modified software as a web service is not distribution, so no source has to be released. The AGPL says that if users interact with your modified version over a network, they are entitled to its source. If you run a SaaS product on top of this software, that clause is the one to read carefully — and it is usually the reason organisations take a commercial licence.
    How is the AUD 50,000 threshold measured?
    Annual income of the organisation, not revenue attributable to the software. It is a deliberately low-friction test: if you are small enough that working it out is annoying, you are small enough to be on the free tier.
    What do the commercial tiers cost?
    Tiered by organisation size, quoted per enquiry. There is no published price list because the catalogue spans an operating system, a decompiler, a numerics library and an anonymity transport — and what you need from each differs enormously. Email with what you want to use and roughly how big you are, and you will get a number rather than a discovery call.
    Can we get a licence for one project rather than all of them?
    Yes. Per-project licences are the normal case; catalogue-wide is available if you want it.
    Is any of this legal advice?
    No. This page is a plain-language summary written by the author of the software, not a lawyer. The AGPL-3.0 text and the commercial licence agreement are what actually bind. If the distinction matters to your organisation, have your own counsel read them.
