Chemistry & products

A bar documented the way an engineer documents a factory

Recipe content tells you what to mix. The documentation that actually keeps a bar running looks nothing like it: infusion timings, batch ratios, prep rhythms, quality checkpoints and a platform abstraction that lets a seasonal menu change without re-deriving everything underneath it. This folder is that second kind of document.

What it is

Four native-botanical bases driving every infusion, syrup and bitters on the menu, with explicit timings, batch ratios, QC checkpoints and shift rhythms.

The platform is four Australian native-botanical bases — Coastal, Bush Orchard, Outback Heat, Native Forest — from which every spirit infusion, syrup, tincture and bitters derives. Above them sit four signature series of three drinks each, with a zero-proof mirror per series, so a seasonal rework changes the finished goods without disturbing the intermediates.

Timings and ratios are specified rather than implied: Coastal Gin infuses 48 hours, Bush Vodka 24, Spiced Rum 72; Native Orgeat is a three-day process; syrups are 1:1 by default with Spiced Forest at 2:1 Demerara; garnish dehydration runs at 57 °C for 12 hours. The prep workflow is organised by shift, day, week and month, with an explicit QC checkpoint — taste all syrups daily — and weekend essential-prep-only rules.

Two complete bitters fabrication specs run in parallel, a 500 ml template and a 375 ml alternative on a 21-day simplified timeline, both with phased infusion (primary days 1–10, aromatics 11–17, finishing 18+) at ≥60% ABV with water dilution to land the tincture strength. A separate umami, marine and ferment lane specifies 2-hour mushroom stock, 4-hour fat-wash protocols, a salmon-oil cap of three drops per drink, and 24-hour cold dashi with a 48-hour use window.

Why this is on a research shelf. It is the same move as the defence portfolio and the depot-delivery framework: take a domain that is usually documented as a list of outputs, and document the system that produces them instead. Bases, intermediates, finished goods, QC checkpoints and shift rhythms is factory-floor thinking applied to a bar, and it is the reason this reads as engineering rather than as recipes.

Methods

How it works

  • Base / intermediate / finished-goods hierarchy. Four bases feed every intermediate, which feed the menu — so a menu change does not ripple downward.
  • Explicit batch specification. Every infusion and syrup carries a timing and a ratio, which is what makes the output reproducible across staff.
  • Phased bitters infusion. Primary, aromatic and finishing windows separated so each ingredient class extracts in its own phase.
  • Operational rhythm. Shift, day, week and month cadences with named QC checkpoints and reduced-scope weekend rules.
Stated limitations

What it does not do

Taken from the folder’s own README. Nothing here has been softened.

  • This is operational documentation for a venue that does not exist. No bar has run it.
  • The Australian native botanicals it depends on — green ant gin, riberry, native orgeat inputs — have supply and cost constraints the documents do not price.
  • No costing, no yield analysis, no waste figures — the numbers a real bar would need next to these.
  • Food-safety compliance for the fat-wash, dashi and fermentation protocols is not addressed against any jurisdiction’s requirements.
  • Alcohol-serving regulation, licensing and responsible-service obligations are out of scope entirely.
