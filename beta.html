Help us find what’s broken

Seven pieces of software are finished enough to be useful and rough enough to still surprise us. We would rather hear about that from you now than from a stranger later. There is no signup form and no account — you send an email, we send you the software and a short note on what would help most.

What this actually involves

Being a tester here means using it and saying what happened

No test scripts to follow and no forms to fill in. Use it for something you genuinely care about, and tell us where it got in your way.

What we ask of you
  • Try it on a real task, not a toy one — that is where the problems live
  • Tell us when it broke, and roughly what you were doing at the time
  • Say when something is confusing. Confusing is a bug
  • Do not put it in front of customers or patients yet. It is not ready for that

“I could not work out how to start” is one of the most useful messages you can send. It means the documentation is wrong, and that is our problem to fix.

What you get back
  • The software, free, and free to keep using under its open licence
  • Your name in the credits, if you want it there
  • Real influence on what gets built next — there is no committee to go through
  • A straight answer every time, including “you are right, that is bad”

If you are a business weighing this up for real work, say so. We would rather talk about whether it genuinely fits than collect another signup.

The honest caveats

This is unpaid, and we will not pretend otherwise
There is no fee and no voucher. What is on offer is early access, a say in what gets built, and a direct line to the person building it. If that is not worth your time, that is an entirely reasonable conclusion.
It will break, and some of it will be your fault to find
That is the point of a beta. Nothing here has been through an outside security audit, so please do not put it anywhere that real harm follows from it going wrong — not with patient data, not in production, not in court.
You keep your data, and we do not want it
The software runs on your machine. Nothing reports back to us, and this website has no trackers, cookies or analytics of any kind. If you send us a file to reproduce a bug, we use it for that and nothing else.
Free for most people, paid only above a threshold
All of it is open source and free for personal use, charity, education and organisations under AUD 50,000 a year. Above that there is a commercial licence. Testing it costs nothing either way — see licensing for the detail.
One person answers the email
Imortek is Odin Loch, working out of Sydney with a day job. Replies are usually quick but not instant, and there is no support desk behind them. The upside is that whoever answers you can change the code that afternoon.
