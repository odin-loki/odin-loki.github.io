Licensing

Free for almost everyone. Paid where it pays.

Everything Imortek publishes is source-available under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later. If you are a person, a charity, a school, or an organisation earning under 50,000 Australian dollars (AUD) a year, that costs you nothing. Above that threshold, or if you need to keep your modifications private, there is a tiered commercial licence.

In plain English

Six ways an organisation can work with Imortek

Licensing is one of them, and it is the one this page goes on to explain in detail. Here are the other five first, because most people arrive with a problem rather than a legal question.

01

Technology deployment

Take something already built and put it to work where you are. The systems on this site are public, tested and documented, and each page says what its software does today and what it does not. If one of them fits, the work is installation, your own data, and the parts that only break under real conditions.

02

Commissioned development

These systems were built to be general. An operational requirement never is. Where something is close to what you need but not aimed at it, it can be adapted — a different data format, a model fitted to your problem, an interface built around how your people actually work. Scoped and quoted before any of it starts.

03

Research partnership

Some of this work is research and is labelled as research. Where an approach looks promising but is unproven, the honest way to settle it is to test it together: you bring the problem and the data, this side brings the implementation and the measurement. Findings are written down either way, negative ones included.

04

Licensing

Put Imortek technology inside your own product or infrastructure. Everything here is AGPL-3.0+ by default, which suits open work but reaches into the code you build around it. A commercial licence removes that reach and the duty to publish your modifications. Per-project is the normal case. The rest of this page is the detail.

05

Investment

There is no team, no runway and no growth plan behind this work, and the about page says so plainly. What money buys here is time and compute. The ParanoidBSD campaign is the public version of that ask — AUD 10,000, all-or-nothing, with every assumption behind the figure laid out and adjustable. For anything larger, email and say what interests you.

06

Technical evaluation

Test it against your own data before anyone signs anything. Beta access is free: no contract, no Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA), no account. You send an email, you get the software and a short note on what would help most. If it falls over on your data, that is the result worth having, and it is the one that changes the software.

Not sure which of these you are after? Say what you are trying to do and roughly how big your organisation is. You will get a straight answer rather than a discovery call — including “that one is not ready for you yet” where that is the truth. Everything below is the licence detail sitting underneath all six.

    The exception

    Upstream code in ParanoidBSD is not relicensed

    ParanoidBSD vendors two upstream trees and ports from both. hbsd/ stays under HardenedBSD’s and FreeBSD’s own terms; kde/ stays under KDE’s. Neither can be relicensed and Imortek does not claim to. Ported files under pbsd/ keep the upstream copyright header they inherited, and that header governs the file — it beats the per-path default.

    LICENSING.md is the authoritative per-path map, and docs/PROVENANCE.md records which modules are ported and which are new PBSD work, so the boundary is checkable rather than asserted. New work — the capability nucleus, the tooling, the compositor and the Aero theme — carries the Imortek dual licence.

    Three carve-outs the audit turned up. Twenty-two ported files inherit the original four-clause Berkeley Software Distribution (BSD) licence, whose advertising clause the AGPL cannot accept, so they are excluded from the AGPL grant and must not enter an AGPL binary. pbsd/zfs/ derives from OpenZFS, which is under the Common Development and Distribution License (CDDL) and has no compatibility route to the AGPL — ZFS stays a separate unmodified module. And the KDE-derived work is compatible only through the “or later” clause, so code under the GNU General Public License version 2.0 alone (GPL-2.0-only) must never enter the tree. All three are listed in NOTICES.md and enforced by a Continuous Integration (CI) check.

    Questions people actually ask

    Before you email

    We are a startup under AUD 50,000. Do we really pay nothing?
    Correct — you use it under AGPL-3.0+ for free. The obligations still apply: publish your modifications under the same dual licence, keep the attribution line, and be aware that offering it over a network counts as distribution under AGPL. When you cross the threshold, talk to us then.
    What does “network use triggers source obligations” actually mean?
    The AGPL closes the gap the GPL leaves open. Under the GPL, running modified software as a web service is not distribution, so no source has to be released. The AGPL says that if users interact with your modified version over a network, they are entitled to its source. If you run a SaaS product on top of this software, that clause is the one to read carefully — and it is usually the reason organisations take a commercial licence.
    How is the AUD 50,000 threshold measured?
    Annual income of the organisation, not revenue attributable to the software. It is a deliberately low-friction test: if you are small enough that working it out is annoying, you are small enough to be on the free tier.
    What do the commercial tiers cost?
    Tiered by organisation size, quoted per enquiry. There is no published price list because the catalogue spans an operating system, a decompiler, a numerics library and an anonymity transport — and what you need from each differs enormously. Email with what you want to use and roughly how big you are, and you will get a number rather than a discovery call.
    Can we get a licence for one project rather than all of them?
    Yes. Per-project licences are the normal case; catalogue-wide is available if you want it.
    Is any of this legal advice?
    No. This page is a plain-language summary written by the author of the software, not a lawyer. The AGPL-3.0 text and the commercial licence agreement are what actually bind. If the distinction matters to your organisation, have your own counsel read them.
