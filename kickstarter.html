The case

Why this needs money, and why it needs this kind of money

Porting a hardened BSD to C++23 is a decade of full-time work done by hand. It is also the kind of task where a language model is genuinely useful — mechanical translation with a strict specification to check against, which is close to the best case for the technology.

The design already reflects that. Deterministic rewrite passes handle everything they safely can, for free. Models are used only on the residue: stubbed inventory entries and the files the deterministic passes refused. Every model output then goes through compile, ASan, UBSan, differential execution and IR comparison before it counts.

That verification is not optional and it is not cheap. Each file is attempted, checked, often rejected and retried. The cost is in the loop, not in a single pass — and the loop is what turns model output into something a security-focused operating system can actually ship.

What this campaign does not fund: salary, hardware, marketing, or anything that is not directly the port. Imortek is one person with a day job as a toolmaker. The ask is for compute.

Risks

What could go wrong

Kickstarter requires a risks section. This one is written to be actually useful.

The port could stall on hard files
Deterministic passes handle the mechanical majority. The residue is, by definition, the part that resisted mechanical translation — and some of it will resist the model too. The verification gates mean those files stay visibly unfinished rather than silently wrong, but they still have to be written by hand, and that is slow.
Provider pricing could move
The budget is denominated in tokens, not dollars, precisely because rates move. A price rise means fewer files per dollar, and the honest response is to report that and slow down — not to drop the verification gates to save money. Dropping the gates would make the whole exercise pointless.
This is one person
Imortek is Odin Loch, working as a toolmaker in Sydney. There is no team, no runway, and no continuity plan beyond the fact that everything is published under an open licence as it is written. If this stops, what exists stays public and forkable. That is the only guarantee that can honestly be offered, so it is the one being offered.
An AI-assisted OS port invites justified scepticism
It should. The mitigation is structural rather than rhetorical: models never self-certify, compile-only is treated as unverified, HardenedBSD is the behavioural specification, and every module needs a provenance entry before it counts as done. You do not have to take that on trust — the gates are in the repository and you can read them before backing anything.
Security software from an unaudited source
PBSD inherits HardenedBSD’s mitigations, which are real and externally reviewed. The new capability nucleus is not externally reviewed and will not be for some time. Until it is, PBSD is a research operating system. Anyone who tells you a fresh capability layer is production-ready on day one is selling something.
